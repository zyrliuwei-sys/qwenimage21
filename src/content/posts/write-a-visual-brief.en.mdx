An image prompt works best when it reads like a short brief to a photographer or art director. Start with what the viewer should notice, then add only the details that change the result.

## Begin with the subject

Name the main subject precisely. “A portrait” leaves many choices open; “a quiet studio portrait of an older woman in a charcoal coat” establishes a person, mood and wardrobe. If one object matters, make it the center of the sentence.

## Set the scene and the light

Describe where the subject is and what illuminates it. A north-facing window, soft reflected daylight or a single warm lamp gives the scene a physical logic. Choose one or two lighting cues that work together.

## Decide what the frame includes

Composition can be simple: close portrait, wide landscape, centered still life or an off-center subject with negative space. State the camera angle or distance when it matters. Leave enough room for the image to surprise you.

## Add texture and restraint

Material details often do more than long style lists. Matte paper, weathered stone, rippled water and brushed metal each suggest a surface that light can describe. End with a brief note on the overall tone.

For example: “A single persimmon on a bare branch above dark water. Soft side light reveals the leaf edges; the reflection fades into gentle ripples. Quiet still life, generous negative space, natural color.”
